    Night one

    Eight ways to keep the first fifty

    Retention on an empty train isn't a notification problem, it's an honesty problem. The app has to be worth opening when it can't do the thing it exists for — and the people who'll fix that are sitting within thirty metres of the person holding the phone.

    01 · DISTRIBUTION

    Never launch to individuals

    One student installing it alone is guaranteed to see nothing. Twenty students from the same hostel installing it in the same week are guaranteed to collide on a train within a fortnight.

    Do thisPick one Lucknow college, get into three of their WhatsApp groups, and launch to all of them on the same day.
    02 · TIMING

    Launch on the weekend everyone travels

    Student travel is spiky, not steady. Semester start, semester end, and the long festival weekends are when a hundred people from one college are on the same three trains.

    Do thisWork backwards from the academic calendar and don't launch on a random Tuesday in the middle of term.
    03 · PRODUCT

    Be useful with zero other users

    The halt guide, the waiting room and the private note all work alone and offline. They're the reason someone opens the app a second time on a night when nobody joined.

    Do thisJudge every new feature by whether it still does something when the user is the only person aboard.
    04 · MECHANIC

    Your growth engine is the seat opposite

    Unlike almost any other social app, your missing users are physically present. The person across the aisle is bored, holding a phone, and going to the same city.

    Do thisMake the join code a single screen that's fast to show someone, and make scanning it drop them straight onto tonight's train.
    05 · MECHANIC

    Let people announce a journey early

    Density has to exist before the train leaves, not after. Someone posting "I'm on 12229 Friday" into a college group three days ahead is worth more than any notification you can send.

    Do thisMake a shareable journey card the main action on an empty train, aimed squarely at WhatsApp.
    06 · WIDENING

    Widen the match when it's thin

    If tonight's train is empty, the honest thing is to show that the route isn't. Other trains, other days, a weekly count — proof of life without pretending anyone is beside you right now.

    Do thisMatch on the train first, then the route, then the week. Narrow it again as density grows.
    07 · DOING IT BY HAND

    Personally onboard the first fifty

    At this size you can message every single user yourself. You'll learn more from ten replies than from any analytics you could afford, and people stay for a founder who wrote to them.

    Do thisMessage every new user the day after their journey and ask one question: did you talk to anyone?
    08 · RESTRAINT

    Go quiet when it's empty

    The instinct is to notify harder when nobody's around. It's the fastest way to get uninstalled. An app that stays silent on a dead night keeps the right to buzz on a live one.

    Do thisSend exactly one notification — when a second person actually joins your train. Nothing else.

    The one thing that would kill it

    Do not seed fake profiles to make an empty train look busy. Every other social product can get away with it for a while; you cannot, because your users can lift their heads and count the people in the coach. One person working out that "Priya, two coaches down" doesn't exist is the end — not of that user, but of every group chat they're in. An honest empty screen is a smaller cost than that.

    The rule the whole app is built on

    Two taps, or nothing

    Everything a person could use to find you in the real world comes from your ticket, and none of it is visible by default. Asking to unlock costs nothing, reveals nothing, and is never announced.

    Visible to everyone onboard

    • First name only
    • One photo, no gallery
    • Two lines you wrote yourself
    • A few tags — what you're into
    • Whether you're open to talking

    Only after you both unlock

    • Coach and seat number
    • College and year
    • Where you're getting off
    • Instagram or phone
    • Hometown
    The data

    A database that empties itself every night

    The thing to hold onto: this is a scratchpad, not a record. Almost every row exists because of one train on one date, and dies shortly after that train arrives. What's left over is tiny — who you are, and who you never want to see again.

    Kept forever

    • users — name, photo, two lines, tags, language, date of birth
    • blocks — who blocked whom
    • reports — who reported whom, and on which journey

    Deleted automatically

    • journeys — train, date, coach, visibility
    • threads and messages
    • unlocks — who asked, whether it was mutual
    • plans — adda, and who joined

    Blocks are the exception that matters. Everything else can vanish with the journey, but if you blocked somebody on the Lucknow Mail in August they must still be invisible to you in December. Blocks outlive the data that created them.

    TableWhat it holdsHow long it lives
    usersFirst name, photo URL, 140-character bio, tags, language, date of birth, account statusUntil they delete it
    blocksblocker → blockedForever
    reportsReporter, reported, journey, reasonKept for review
    journeysUser, service code, travel date, coach, boarding and alighting station, visibility24h after arrival
    threadsKind (one-to-one, coach, whole train), service code, last_message_at9h after the last message
    messagesThread, sender, body, client_idWith the thread
    unlocksThe pair, who asked, whether both didWith the journey
    plansTitle, place, time, host, who joinedWith the journey
    One row's whole life

    From adding a journey to nothing left

    1Journey addedA row appears with an expiry set to arrival plus 24 hours. The halt guide downloads to the phone in the same moment.
    2Second person joinsThe coach thread is created here — not on the first join, because a room with one person in it is worse than no room.
    3Every messagePushes the thread's expiry to nine hours from now. A long conversation keeps itself alive; a dead one starts counting down.
    4Train arrives, lateNothing happens. No timer is tied to the timetable, which is exactly why a four-hour delay breaks nothing.
    5Nine quiet hoursThread and every message in it are deleted. An hourly scheduled job does this — free on both Supabase and Firebase.
    6What survivesYour account, your photo, and any blocks. If you unlocked someone you already have their Instagram, which was the point.
    The whole app, roughly

    One indexed lookup

    Finding who's onboard is a single query against an index on (service_code, travel_date), filtered by visibility and by blocks in both directions. That's the core read path, and it stays cheap because the table only ever holds journeys that haven't expired yet.

    WhatSumSize
    Accounts25,000 users at roughly 500 bytes each12 MB
    PhotosOne each, compressed on the phone to about 40 KB before upload1 GB
    A busy night200 people talking, 40 messages each, deleted the next day1.6 MB
    Messages after a yearNothing accumulates, so this number never grows0

    Photos are your real constraint, not conversations. A free 1 GB bucket runs out at about 25,000 accounts while the message tables sit near empty. When that day comes, move images to a free 10 GB object store and leave the database exactly where it is.

    Offline

    The phone is the source of truth for forty minutes

    WRITE

    Everything lands locally first

    A message is written to the phone's own store and rendered immediately, marked as waiting. The network attempt happens afterwards and is allowed to fail.

    WhyPast Etawah there is no network for about forty minutes. An app that waits for a server there looks broken.
    SYNC

    Every message carries a client id

    The phone generates a UUID for each message before sending. The server treats a repeat of that id as the same message rather than a new one.

    WhyRetrying after a tunnel is then completely safe, and nobody ever sees the same line posted three times.
    CACHE

    Downloaded when the journey is added

    Halt guide, your own journey, your profile and the waiting-room lines all land on the phone while there's still signal at the station.

    WhyThese are the screens that need to work at two in the morning, and they're small enough to ship in a few kilobytes.
    NEVER

    Locked fields never reach the device

    Coach, seat, college and contact are withheld server-side until both unlocks exist. They aren't sent and hidden — they're not sent.

    WhyAnything the phone holds can be read off the phone. The privacy promise has to be enforced before the data leaves.
    Marketing

    Eight ways to get found, none of which cost money

    You aren't buying installs. You're getting into rooms where forty people who travel the same route already talk to each other every day — and those rooms are all on WhatsApp and Instagram.

    01 · THE REAL CHANNEL

    College WhatsApp groups

    This is the distribution network in India and it isn't for sale. Every year group, every hostel floor, every department has one, and a link posted by a member outperforms any ad you could run.

    Do thisFind one person in each year who'll post it, not one person overall. A message from a stranger gets ignored; from a classmate it gets installed.
    02 · REACH

    Local college meme pages

    Lucknow college meme accounts have the exact audience, and their rates are low or nothing for something genuinely local. The content writes itself: eight hours, twelve percent battery, nobody to talk to.

    Do thisGive them the joke, not a poster. Make the app the punchline rather than the subject.
    03 · PEOPLE

    Two ambassadors per college

    Students will do this for status rather than money — a title, early access, and their name attached to something new. Two committed people beat a hundred passive installs.

    Do thisAsk them for one specific thing: post their own journey to their group three days before every trip home.
    04 · PLACE

    Stickers where people are already waiting

    Charbagh's reservation queue, hostel notice boards, the wall by the mess. Nobody in those places has anything to do, which is precisely the state the app is for.

    Do thisPrint the join code from the invite screen. Ask before putting anything on railway property — don't get the app associated with defacement.
    05 · TIMING

    Freshers' week is the whole year

    First-years arriving is the single densest moment: maximum travel, maximum loneliness, and a cohort that will keep travelling this route for three more years.

    Do thisPut everything you have into that fortnight and treat the rest of the year as maintenance.
    06 · STORY

    Say why you built it

    "I was bored and alone on the Lucknow Mail and built something about it" is a better pitch than any feature list, and it's free. It also makes people forgive an empty first night.

    Do thisPost it once on r/lucknow and r/indianrailways with an honest note that it's new and probably empty on your train.
    07 · LOOP

    Ask for the return journey

    Everybody going home is coming back. Getting the return trip added at the end of the first one doubles usage per person and costs nothing to build.

    Do thisAsk once, on arrival, while they're still holding the phone at New Delhi.
    08 · VOICES

    Small local creators, not big ones

    A Lucknow student with eight thousand followers is worth more here than someone national with a million, because their audience is literally on your train.

    Do thisOffer nothing but the app and a genuine ask. At this size, national reach is wasted spend anyway.

    How you describe it decides what it becomes

    Don't market this as meeting strangers — it worries students and alarms the parents paying their fees. Don't market it as dating either, because you'll get dating users and they will drive out everybody else; the people you acquire first define what the app turns into, permanently. Lead with the boredom rather than the loneliness: nobody downloads something that tells them they have no friends, but everybody recognises eight hours with a dying battery and nothing to do. And stay specific — "for the Lucknow Mail" is a real thing a real person is on tonight, while "for travellers" is nothing at all.

    Scale

    One route, without locking anybody out

    You're right, and it changes what I said earlier. Restricting the app to a corridor would mean a person who finds you on Twitter hits a wall, you can't show it to anyone outside Uttar Pradesh, and every new city needs a switch flipped. So don't restrict the app. Concentrate the marketing instead, and let the product go anywhere.

    01 · THE CORRECTION

    Focus the effort, not the software

    Anyone in the country can install Safar and add any train. What stays concentrated is where you spend your fortnight of promotion — because density is what makes it work, and density only happens where you push.

    Do thisShip nationally, market to Lucknow. Nobody outside ever sees a restriction, and nobody inside sees an empty train.
    02 · TRAVELS FREE

    Most of it already works anywhere

    Halt times, platforms and durations come from the public timetable, so that screen works on any train in India from day one. The waiting room and the private note need no other users at all.

    Do thisHand-write the halt tips for your route only. Everywhere else shows the times without the commentary — thinner, not broken.
    03 · HONESTY

    An off-route screen, not a wall

    Someone from Bhopal gets told the truth — this route isn't live, here's the count, here's what turns it on — plus the parts that work regardless. See the Off route mode in the prototype.

    Do thisMake the threshold public. "Twenty-three more people" is a target somebody can act on; "coming soon" is not.
    04 · EXPANSION

    Grow on a hub, never at random

    Every student corridor you add next should end at Delhi — Patna, Kota, Varanasi, Gorakhpur. Each new route reuses the Delhi-side population you already have instead of starting from nothing.

    Do thisLet the waiting counts choose the order. That list is real demand data, and it costs nothing to collect.
    05 · THE TWITTER ANSWER

    Demo the mechanic, not the crowd

    What's worth showing has nothing to do with how many people are aboard: the blurred profile resolving on a mutual unlock, the yellow screen when somebody joins an empty train, the halt guide at 2am with no signal.

    Do thisRecord those three. Nobody demonstrates a messaging app by filming an empty inbox.

    The one thing genuinely lost by going national early is the excuse. With no restriction, an empty train is the product's fault rather than the map's — which is exactly why the night-one screen had to be built first, and why it was worth building properly.

    Safety

    Moderation when there is no moderator

    You have no team and no budget, and the report that matters will arrive at three in the morning somewhere past Tundla. So reporting cannot mean joining a queue. Every report has to solve the reporter's problem instantly, by itself — and a human looks at it whenever you next have time.

    TriggerWhat happensDecided by
    1 reportThat person is hidden from the reporter permanently, in both directions, on every future journeyInstantly, automatic
    2 reportsDifferent people, same journey — they stop appearing to anyone on that trainInstantly, automatic
    3 reportsDifferent people, any time — account suspended and invisible everywhereAutomatic, you review later
    New accountUnder three journeys and already reported once — suspended straight awayAutomatic, you review later
    Anything involving a minorImmediate suspension, no thresholdAutomatic, you review same day

    Prevention is cheaper than review. There is no free way to check a PNR — the official railway API is expensive and restricted, and the scrapers that wrap it are unreliable — so version one does not verify tickets at all. Anyone can claim any train. Two limits carry the weight instead: a new account can start at most three conversations per journey, and cannot send links until it has a few journeys behind it. Those two rules kill spraying and scams without a single human decision, and both are enforced in the database rather than the app.

    The rule that's specific to a train

    On a dating app the person you report cannot reach you. On the Lucknow Mail they are twenty metres away for the next six hours. That single fact should shape the whole flow: never tell the reported person a report happened while the journey is still running, make the hiding silent and mutual so it simply looks as though the other person left, and hold any suspension notice until after Delhi. A retaliation risk that most apps can treat as theoretical is, here, a person walking down the aisle.

    One thing to check before you ship: the prototype shows 139 for the railway helpline and 112 for emergencies. Those match my understanding of the current Indian numbers, but a wrong number inside a safety flow is worse than no number at all — confirm both against an official source, and consider whether to surface the RPF number separately.

    What English-only costs, so it's on the record

    Dropping the language picker halves the copy work and removes a decision from the welcome screen, which is the right call for a first version. The thing to keep an eye on is who it quietly selects for: a student who thinks in Hindi is more likely to be the one travelling alone and knowing nobody in Delhi, and an English-only interface skews the early user base toward metro, English-medium students — the people who are already the least lonely on that train. It is not a reason to build Hindi now. It is a reason to watch, once there are real users, whether the ones signing up are the ones this was built for.
